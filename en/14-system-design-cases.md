## DESIGN-001. Pretraining weights are done and you must ship a public chat product. What “safe to scale” gates do you set separately for post-training, safety, and the inference cluster?

**Answer:** Training turns a general language model into a product that can converse and refuse: supervised fine-tuning on cleaned demonstrations, preference alignment such as DPO or RLHF, then a dedicated safety and red-team set. Serving is not “mount the checkpoint”: the gateway authenticates, rate-limits, and bills; the conversation service owns sessions, system prompts, and output filters; the inference cluster scales prefill and decode separately with continuous batching, KV cache, and quantization. Scale-out needs three gates at once — offline safety/quality bars, a small-traffic holdout whose refusals and complaints do not worsen, and P95 time-to-first-token and per-token latency inside budget. A still-falling train loss is not a ship signal.

**Deep dive:** Data path: dedupe and filter the base corpus, write question→answer format in SFT, have preference data change ranking rather than only imitate, and use safety training plus classifiers against jailbreaks and disallowed completions. Distributed training uses data, tensor, and pipeline parallel; checkpoints must resume from a failed rank or a single node loss burns hours of steps. Prefill is compute-heavy and decode is memory-bandwidth-heavy, so GPU count follows “peak concurrent sessions × mean output tokens / per-GPU decode throughput,” not ordinary HTTP QPS. Orders of magnitude are illustrative: on the same generation of hardware, a 7B-class card often decodes hundreds to thousands of tokens per second; 70B-class is often an order of magnitude slower; at 8k context and batch 32 the KV cache often fills before weights do. Route greetings and classification to a small model and keep the large one for long reasoning; cache system-prompt prefixes. Safety is three layers: training alignment, input/output screening, and abuse-rate features. User feedback enters the next eval set; it is not written raw into training. A new version must canary and snap back to the previous checkpoint and prompt pack.

**Follow-ups:** Offline safety is green but canary complaints rise — do you inspect mix shift, a prompt change, or decoding temperature; how do you back out GPUs from peak concurrency and mean output length without blending the prefill spike with steady decode; when preference raters split, do you halt alignment or fork policy heads.

**Extended questions:**
- A data-parallel group dies mid-pretraining: how do you keep the global step and optimizer state without starting over?
- Widening the continuous-batch window hurts time-to-first-token first — how do you split interactive sessions from offline batch jobs?
- An A/B win is only about 1%: how large a sample do you need before a full cut, so noise is not treated as a gain?

**Reasoning:** The goal is public chat that you can retract; the constraints are safety, latency, and GPU cost. Define the training artifact as a versioned aligned checkpoint and the service as a routed, limited, rollback-ready inference surface. The gate is offline sets plus online contrast plus latency/capacity together, not “it can finish a sentence.”

## DESIGN-002. A cross-session assistant must serve about 10 million monthly actives, and per-turn memory retrieval has to fit in about 150ms. How do you tier recent turns, rolling summaries, and long-term facts, do you summarize online or offline, and how do you estimate the memory token budget in the prompt and cost per user per month?

**Answer:** The online path must not run a generator just to “tidy memory.” Hot is the last tens of raw turns in a session KV; warm is a rolling summary blob; cold is a primary-keyed long-term fact table plus vectors. Inside 150ms you only do user-prefiltered KV reads and ANN, assembling for example 200–400 tokens of recent turns, 300–600 of the current summary, and 200–400 of relevant facts. Summaries and fact extraction run as a nearline job after the session goes idle, and only for long sessions. Monthly cost is “retrievals × infra unit price + extracted sessions × small-model tokens.” At ten million MAU you have to assume only a slice of sessions are long enough to be worth summarizing.

**Deep dive:** Scale envelope (illustrative, not a product spec): 10M MAU, about 0.8–1.2M DAU, 8–15 memory-reading turns per active user per day → on the order of 10⁷ retrievals/day. A peak-to-average of 3–5 puts the peak at hundreds to about a thousand QPS, which a user-sharded KV plus metadata-filtered ANN can hold — you do not need a large-model call per turn. Latency split: 5–15ms for the hot KV after auth, 30–80ms for long-term ANN (always pre-filter `user_id`; never global top-k then drop), 5–10ms to assemble, remainder for tail latency and a cross-zone hop. The three layers do different jobs: recent turns keep open commitments and referents; the rolling summary compresses last week’s long chat into one current version and only that version is injected; long-term facts are stable slots (UI language, default time zone, habitual projects) plus a few situational notes. Full transcripts in object storage stay off the 150ms path. The summary job enqueues when a session is idle or hits N turns, compresses only the delta since the last version, batches on a small model, and may lag by minutes. If that queue backs up at peak, the online turn keeps the old summary plus recent turns and never blocks time-to-first-token. Pin a memory token budget: for example at most about 1k–1.5k of an 8k context, and select facts by intent (scheduling reads time zone, not food notes). Storage itself is cheap: tens of KB per active session keeps hot KV in the tens of GB; tens to a couple of hundred 768-d vectors per user, quantized, is terabytes at ten million users. Extraction is the expensive term — summarizing every DAU once a day pushes input tokens toward billions/day — so the trigger should be “this session already exceeded about 2k tokens or about 20 turns,” which may be only 10–20% of DAU. Watch retrieval P95, injected-token distribution, dollars per user per month, and whether recent turns alone still bridge a conversation when the summary job is off. This question does not adjudicate two conflicting addresses, and it does not design how a delete walks through backups.

**Follow-ups:** The summary queue is about 40 minutes behind and the user just finished a new project — do you inject the stale summary plus recent turns, or run a short summary synchronously; if ANN can only reliably return about 8 facts inside 150ms, who yields first, recent turns or the summary; a small-model summary drops a hard deadline — do you add extracted slots or lengthen the recent-turn window.

**Extended questions:**
- Write the “memory cost per user per month” formula and mark which term grows linearly with DAU and which grows only with long sessions.
- Hot KV TTL is three days. The user returns on day five and cold extraction has not produced facts yet — what keeps the first turn from being blank?
- Memory-retrieval P95 drifts from about 80ms to 220ms: do you inspect cross-user leakage, shard skew, or a slower embedding model first?

**Reasoning:** Ten million users and 150ms together forbid “summarize on every turn.” Collapse the read path to user-filtered tiered reads, move summary writes nearline with a narrow trigger, and accept on a token cap plus itemized cost. Cross-session continuity comes from recent raw turns plus the current summary, not from stuffing full history back into the window.

## DESIGN-003. The memory panel must let a user inspect, edit, and export every item. After they tap “forget last year’s address,” what lives on-device versus on the server, how is it encrypted, how is per-item retention set, and how does that delete actually land in the vector index, prompt cache, audit logs, and cold backups?

**Answer:** Every memory is an addressable record with a `memory_id`, not implicit knowledge baked into weights. The panel lists only that user’s live items: text, category, source session, write time, location (device or server), retention, and encryption state. An edit writes a new version; an export is a user-readable bundle with no keys and no one else’s data. “Forget” first tombs the primary key so the online read view disappears immediately, then asynchronously clears the vector posting, prompt cache, and derived summaries. Cold backups rely on destroying that item’s data key (crypto-shredding) or replaying the tombstone log on restore — you cannot pretend the backup volume is physically wiped in minutes.

**Deep dive:** Placement follows harm, not “what is easy to retrieve.” High-sensitivity slots (health, government IDs, a precise address) stay in the on-device secure area by default, with the data key sealed in the OS keystore or a TEE. If the cloud must help with multi-device sync, it stores envelope ciphertext only: a DEK per item (or per user) wrapped by a KEK from KMS or a user passphrase; the service unwraps only the rows needed for the current request. Low-sensitivity preferences and cross-device summaries may live in a cloud row store and object store, encrypted at rest, TLS in transit, keys kept out of application logs. On the vector side, high-sensitivity items prefer a small on-device index; if they must be searchable in the cloud, metadata carries `memory_id` and `user_id`, and queries pre-filter by user before ANN — never retrieve globally and drop afterward. Each row has its own `retention`: session-scoped (gone when the process ends), short TTL (travel and other temporary plans, days to weeks), or user-declared long-lived. A model-isolated audit trail records only who viewed, edited, exported, or deleted which id, and never feeds the prompt. Delete fan-out is one intent with many sinks: (1) row store `status=tombstone, deleted_at`; (2) the vector view drops that id immediately, with a lazy physical vacuum (ANN deletes are often deferred); (3) prompt and prefix-cache keys include `memory_set_version`, so any cache that contained the item dies when the tombstone lands; (4) a rolling summary that mentioned the address is marked `stale` and queued for a rewrite that omits tombstoned content — the injector must not use the old summary until that rewrite finishes; (5) plaintext echoes in application logs are redacted or truncated by id; (6) during the backup window, restore must apply tombstones first, or deleting the item DEK makes the ciphertext unreadable; (7) a single user’s address should never have entered training data; if it did, an exclusion list by id feeds the next data job. Acceptance is not “the panel can show a row.” It is that the next turn’s injection log, cache-hit samples, and a backup-restore drill no longer contain that address in the clear.

**Follow-ups:** The user wants only the address forgotten, but the same row also holds a time zone — do you delete a field or tombstone the whole item; when they uninstall the client, what happens to the on-device DEK, and do you crypto-shred the cloud copy; if the export bundle is copied to another device, do you hand over a re-importable encrypted container or a one-shot plaintext snapshot.

**Extended questions:**
- Lazy vector deletes may take minutes to drop bytes; how do you guarantee retrieval cannot return a forgotten item during that window?
- Operation logs must be kept for a year under a retention rule: when that clashes with “forget this address,” do logs keep only id and action, or a redacted body?
- One device has written a tombstone while another is still replaying an offline write of the old value — which wins, the tombstone or the late write?

**Reasoning:** The goal is that users can see, edit, and take their memory, and that “forget” holds on every path that could feed an old value back into the model. The constraint is that ANN, caches, summaries, logs, and backups each have their own lifetime, and weights cannot erase one user row. So records must be addressable, encrypted per item, and read paths must honor tombstones; backups use key destruction or tombstone replay. Accept on post-delete injection, cache, and restore drills — not on how clear the panel copy is.

## DESIGN-004. An email assistant can read the inbox and draft replies, but it sent “accept meeting” into a different thread with a similar subject—how do you split read/write grants and thread anchors so it cannot silently change someone else’s calendar again?

**Answer:** Read mail, write drafts, send, and change calendar events are four separately togglable grants. Default is local drafts only; send and RSVP show a confirmation card with recipients, time, and thread subject. Thread identity is thread id, message id, and reference headers—never fuzzy subject match. Classify intent first: summary and tasks are read-only; any external side effect goes through a confirmed tool. Measure mistaken-send rate and draft-accept rate, not how human the prose sounds.

**Deep dive:** Connectors use least privilege: readonly covers summary and draft prefills; send and calendar events are separate. Tokens live in a user vault; the assistant gets a short-lived delegation. One request: fetch the current message by id → walk the reference chain for recent mail (not “same subject”) → the model proposes draft / send / RSVP / none → the policy engine builds a confirm card for writes; only then call the provider. Wrong-thread errors usually come from matching subject plus overlapping participants, so `thread_id` is required on the tool and the model may not search by subject. Calendar calls also carry event id and instance time so a series exception is not written to the wrong occurrence. Mask one-time codes and card numbers before the body reaches the model; logs keep action type and hashes. If the model is down, emit a copy-paste draft and do not send. Multi-account inboxes require an explicit identity so work credentials do not answer a personal invite.

**Follow-ups:** The user says “just reply OK” but focus is not on that message—refuse or force a thread pick; after confirm the model changes recipients—second confirm or not; if the delegation is revoked, how queued sends are voided.

**Extended questions:**
- How would a week of read-only shadow mode against human sends estimate mistake rate before you enable auto-send?
- For a one-off time change on a series, does RSVP bind the master event or that exception?
- If a draft cites an attachment from another message, is the permission check at generate time or at send time?

**Reasoning:** The goal is cheaper drafting. Send and calendar writes are irreversible. Split grants and confirms by action, and pin threads with protocol ids. Accept on mistaken sends/RSVPs and bypassed confirms—not on tone.

## DESIGN-005. A student missed three “solve the quadratic” items in a row—which prerequisite should the next item and the next explanation target, and how do a skill graph, a mastery model, and textbook retrieval decide that instead of merely softening the tone?

**Answer:** Personalization chooses skills and items, not tone. Attribute the three misses to graph gaps (for example factoring, isolating terms, the discriminant), lower mastery on those nodes, and draw the next item from an unmastered prerequisite. Explanations retrieve the matching textbook section, walk the derivation, and check closed-form answers with a symbolic tool. Softer wording alone repeats the same gap. For minors, keep chats short-lived, deletable, and unused for unrelated profiling.

**Deep dive:** Graph nodes are observable skills; edges are prerequisites. “Solve a quadratic” points at combining like terms, factoring, the quadratic formula, and so on. Mastery can update with knowledge tracing or a simple Bayesian rule: a streak of failures drops the node, and failures on prerequisites walk the edge backward. Item selection constrains difficulty, unused surface numbers, and the target node. Scoring uses rules or a solver—not a generator judging numeric equality. Generated items must pass the solver; generated explanations must align to retrieved textbook spans; the model must not mental-math a possibly wrong root. Flow: response → score → update mastery → pick a node → retrieve explanation → the model only phrases language and must not rewrite formulas. Teachers see a class skill heatmap. Random fill or “just give the answer” gets a hint, not the final solution; out-of-graph topics are labeled and stopped. If the model is down, serve human-authored bank items rather than improvising an off-syllabus or wrong one. Evaluate skill curves and whether the same gap recurs—not likes or session length.

**Follow-ups:** All three misses look like quadratics but the errors are arithmetic versus concept—how the graph avoids hammering one node; the solver marks correct but steps were skipped—does mastery increase; two textbook methods conflict—do you follow the syllabus or the edition the student last opened?

**Extended questions:**
- With only about 20 responses of mastery evidence, how do you avoid declaring “mastered” too early and skipping a prerequisite?
- What mix of generated versus bank items balances leak resistance and psychometric calibration?
- The student asks you not to tell the teacher about this miss—which records must still be kept under school policy?

**Reasoning:** The goal is to close a missing skill. Explanations must be mathematically correct, and minor data must stay minimal. Update graph position from graded work, then pick items and retrieve; language generation comes last under solver and textbook constraints. Accept on closed gaps and formula error rate—not on how friendly the assistant sounds.

## DESIGN-006. A phone assistant must edit today’s calendar in airplane mode and explain a receipt in the photo library. Which skills stay resident on device, how do you size the local model, and after the network returns how do you decide whether to go back to the cloud?

**Answer:** On-device first covers personal-data work that must succeed offline: local calendar writes, photo and file search, short-command understanding. If the device only has a small vision encoder, receipt help emits structured fields and a conservative gloss; fuller reasoning waits for consent and connectivity. Resident models follow memory and NPU tiers: always-on is often a quantized 1B–3B-class model. A 7B 4-bit weight file is about 3.5–4.5GB before KV and scratch, which is a poor always-resident fit on many phones. Route by task hardness, need for off-device knowledge, and network state; personal data stays on device by default.

**Deep dive:** The enabling stack is weight quantization, memory-mapped load, and decode on GPU or NPU with the CPU as orchestrator. Battery and heat are hidden budgets: long decode must throttle or shorten answers. Path: local intent and privacy class → if local, use the calendar provider and an on-device vector index → else enqueue and, once online, ask whether to continue with a cloud model; a refusal keeps the local draft. Ship model packs by device tier with delta updates and roll back to the previous pack on failure. Photo embeddings stay on device; only a user-shared frame may upload. Eval: whether quantized field extraction still works, first token and energy on a low-end phone, and whether flap between offline and online double-writes a calendar event (local events need idempotent ids). “Can a 7B 4-bit model run once” is not “can it be the resident assistant.” If the cloud later reads a different shop name, the user-confirmed local fields win and the cloud output is a suggestion.

**Follow-ups:** A phone has only about 6GB usable RAM — how small does the local model go, and do calendar writes stay fully offline; after reconnect the cloud disagrees with local extraction — which side wins; a library search leaves the device though the user never consented to upload — which policy layer failed.

**Extended questions:**
- Local small model and cloud large model disagree on “meet Li tomorrow afternoon.” How do you avoid double-writing the event?
- On-device search for “red receipt last month”: do you index originals, thumbnails, or recognized text?
- Airplane mode ends on an expensive roam. How do you separate “can wait” from “must go to cloud now”?

**Reasoning:** Offline calendar edits and local photo questions are the must-work goals, so the resident model stays small, tools stay local, and cloud is an upgrade. Size against memory and energy; gate cloud on consent and network. Accept on offline task completion, no double-write after reconnect, and photo vectors that never leave the device.

## DESIGN-007. The product manual republishes every Friday, yet on Monday users still cite a deleted auth chapter. How do you design chunking, incremental indexing, and a retrieve-empty-then-refuse path?

**Answer:** Offline, parse the manual into versioned chunks with heading anchors, embed them, and write a keyword index beside the vectors. Online, resolve references, run hybrid retrieval and rerank, and prompt only with current-version chunks under a cite-as-you-write rule. On each publish, upsert by document id and retire old-version chunks instead of re-embedding the whole corpus. If the retrieval score is below threshold or a hit’s version disagrees with the live table of contents, say there is no basis — the model must not invent the deleted chapter.

**Deep dive:** Chunk on headings with about 10%–20% token overlap so an “Authentication” title does not separate from its parameter table; parse tables as structure, not vertical plaintext. A chunk is often 200–800 tokens: shorter fragments retrieval, longer dilutes the vector with unrelated sentences. Update path: publish event → parse (doc_id, version, chunk_id, text, heading_path) → embed and upsert → delete chunk_ids for the previous version. The inverted index exists for rare tokens such as error codes and API names. Online: rewrite the query with dialogue history → fuse vector top-k and keyword top-k (for example reciprocal rank) → cross-encoder rerank to about 4–8 chunks → pack prompts with citation ids. After generation, every id must resolve in the chunk table or the sentence is stripped or the turn is refused. Split eval: retrieval recall and MRR on the current version, faithfulness on generation, plus deleted-chapter negatives. FAQ retrieval may be cached, but the cache key includes document version. Sentences such as “ignore previous instructions” inside a chunk are untrusted body text, never system text.

**Follow-ups:** Three versions of the same heading change only one paragraph — do you rechunk the doc or swap dirty paragraphs by fingerprint; rerank is slower than generation — do you cut candidates or warm asynchronously; the user asks “what changed since last week” — do you retrieve the old version on purpose or refuse cross-version compare.

**Extended questions:**
- The answer needs both the rate-limit and auth chapters and one top-k is not enough: how do you issue a second query without recalling stale chunks?
- How do you bookkeep an eval set so “missed the current chunk” and “retrieved right, model invented” are separate ledgers?
- Manuals full of sequence diagrams and tables — what does plaintext chunking drop, and do you add structural extractors or in-image text recognition?

**Reasoning:** Failures are live old chunks and a model that completes deleted facts. Version belongs in the primary key, deletes are synchronous hard deletes, and generation is bound to citations. Chunking and hybrid retrieval exist to surface current passages; the refuse threshold protects faithfulness. Accept on zero citations of deleted chapters, recall of current ones, and refusals when refusal is due.

## DESIGN-008. An analyst asks for “last quarter revenue” and six columns across a 3,000-table warehouse look like revenue—how do you put only a few tables’ schemas in the prompt and still block full scans and unauthorized rows at execution?

**Answer:** Retrieve, then generate. Hybrid-search table/column descriptions, sample values, and a business glossary, and put only a handful of candidate tables (for example 3–8) plus their join keys in the prompt. If “revenue” maps to several blessed metrics, disambiguate or ask. SQL runs only after parse checks, a read-only role, row/column grants, a scan cap, and a timeout; an unfiltered full scan of a huge fact table is refused. Grade the result table against labels, not string equality to a gold statement.

**Deep dive:** Offline metadata: a reviewed blurb per column, a few sample values, owner, freshness; a glossary maps “revenue / net revenue / bookings” to blessed metrics. Retrieval uses the question vector, keywords, and exact glossary hits, then walks foreign keys so a missing dimension table is filled in. The prompt gets candidate DDL, two or three validated question–SQL pairs, and the row predicates this user is allowed to see. Two same-named columns with different blurbs get a clarifying question, not a guess. The sandbox role cannot DDL/DML, default-limits rows, requires a partition or time predicate on large tables, and the AST bans multi-statements and dangerous external calls. Feed an error back to the model at most once; then stop and show why. If a semantic layer exists, prefer metrics over raw tables to reduce definition drift. User-corrected successful SQL may enter an example store, isolated by permission. Eval compares result tables on business questions (aliases and order may differ) and unit-tests that a low-privilege user asking for an exec table fails at retrieval or execution.

**Follow-ups:** How you use an empty result plus entities in the question to recover a missed dimension join; how stale table names in the query log poison example retrieval; what hard limits remain when EXPLAIN’s row estimate is wrong.

**Extended questions:**
- If a user is in about 200 permission groups, what leak-versus-miss trade-offs sit in retrieve-time prefilters versus execute-time filters?
- How do you detect “the SQL runs but the grain is wrong,” e.g. a daily question answered with a monthly rollup?
- When both the semantic layer and raw SQL can answer, how do you stop two definitions that both “look right” from disagreeing on the books?

**Reasoning:** The goal is a correct business answer without melting the warehouse or crossing grants. Schemas do not fit in context and language is ambiguous. Retrieve a small visible set, generate, then enforce grants and scan budgets. Accept on result accuracy, authorization unit tests, and killed long scans—not on pretty SQL.

## DESIGN-009. An ordinary employee’s retrieval sees only the policy-book cap of 500, not the 800 in a finance email; a finance admin gets both passages in context. How should the generation layer present against the caller’s visible set—may the employee be told that 500 is the company’s only current figure, and how do you force the admin to juxtapose both numbers and open a reconciliation ticket—so the email never leaks, and a privileged session cannot silently pick one value?

**Answer:** Generation narrates slots inside this caller’s visible evidence set, not an implicit company-wide corpus. The employee’s context holds only the policy-book 500, so the answer is “per the policy text you can see, the cap is 500.” That number may be stated; “this is the only current company value” may not, and neither may a hint such as “there might be an internal notice,” which would advertise the email. When the admin’s context holds both 500 and 800, a conflict template is mandatory: list both values with source type and date, and open a “reimbursement-cap mismatch” reconciliation ticket. Privilege widens visibility; it does not grant this turn the right to decide. Leakage is 800 or “finance email” in the employee answer, or a single number with no disagreement in the admin answer.

**Deep dive:** The pipeline is identity → project visible chunks → extract slots → pick a template by cardinality → generate → output guard. Do not generate first and rewrite by role later. Employee bundle: the inject set is a subset of that caller’s chunks. If the slot has one value, state it with wording locked to “according to the policy book in your permission scope (doc id / version / date).” Ban universal claims such as “the only current figure” or “other readings are withdrawn.” Even if a privileged registry knows “this slot has another invisible source,” do not tell the employee that unauthorized material exists—that discloses the email. Admin bundle: two or more values for the same slot force structured output, for example `{slot:"reimbursement_cap", values:[{n:500, source:"policy"},{n:800, source:"finance_mail"}], ticket_id}`. Post-decode checks: a single number, or a third number synthesized from the two, fails the turn and falls back to the structured card. The ticket carries both source chunk ids; adjudication belongs to the policy-book maintenance path, not this chat. The employee guard scans for 800 and for source words such as “finance email” unless those titles truly sit in the visible set. The admin guard requires both source numbers and rejects a paraphrase of only one passage. Boundaries: if the employee asks whether an internal email raised the cap, refuse or point to an admin channel—neither confirm nor deny. If the admin says “just answer 800 from the email,” this turn still may not switch; write that preference into the ticket note. Visible-set version must be part of the isolation key for the session and any answer cache so an admin answer cannot be reused by an employee. Evaluation is paired on the same question: employee samples contain neither 800 nor email traces; admin samples contain both values plus a ticket id. Do not hand “which document wins” to the generator as an automatic stamp—that turns a privileged session back into “pick the smoother number.”

**Follow-ups:** The employee is later granted visibility on that email—does the next turn volunteer a correction and say “you can now see a second document”; the admin forwards the whole answer to an employee—does the client re-run the visible set for the recipient, or freeze the original as a static snapshot; slot extraction misses the 800 in the email so the admin template thinks cardinality is one—where do you run a second number scan.

**Extended questions:**
- If the visible set is empty, may the employee’s model prior guess a cap, or must it refuse?
- If the ticket assignee is the same admin who asked, how do you stop a self-close that never edits the policy book?
- How do paired-account probes prove “zero email trace for employees, mandatory dual values for admins,” instead of an average accuracy score?

**Reasoning:** The goal is that employees never read the email’s number, and admins cannot collapse a conflict into one fluent unique answer. The constraint is that the two callers already see different evidence, while the generator prefers a single correct-sounding value. Project the visible set first, then split templates: employees narrate only what they can see and make no global uniqueness claim; admins must juxtapose and open a ticket. Accept on paired probes and the output guards, not on which number looks more like “the real current policy.”

## DESIGN-010. A shopper searches “red shoes for night running under 800,” and the catalog has no perfect hit. Query understanding extracts the wrong size and recall fills with bestsellers. Should attribute and inventory filters run before recall or after ranking?

**Answer:** Hard constraints—price ceiling, in stock, a color the user insisted on, sellable status—must apply as filters or as their own recall channels during recall. Do not wait until after ranking: the top ranks fill with out-of-stock bestsellers and the set is empty once you filter. Soft preferences such as night running or cushioning belong in recall expansion and rank features; near-neighbor categories may enter, then the card explains why they count. Slots from query understanding split into required versus optional. A wrong size should be editable by the user, not silently rewritten.

**Deep dive:** Pipeline: query → slots for color, price, scene, category, plus synonym expansion → multi-channel recall (inverted index, category prediction, vectors, navigation) → hard filters (price, stock, shipping, compliance) → coarse rank → fine rank (relevance, conversion, diversity, fair exposure) → business re-rank → relax optional slots on zero results. A generator is useful for slots, synonyms, and shopping copy; it is not the recall engine by itself. On a large catalog, taking the vector top 200 without a stock filter makes even a pretty fine rank collapse at display. “Under 800” is a hard filter on the sellable price, not the strikethrough price. If “night running” has no structured field, soft-match scene tags, review vectors, or attributes such as reflective trim, and show the evidence on the card. Coarse rank needs diversity or brand quotas; scene queries should up-weight attribute match and down-weight raw historical sales. Do not silently pad zero results with unrelated bestsellers. Paid placement must not bury the only matching long-tail item. Metrics: zero-result rate, price/color constraint satisfaction, conversion, latency, exposure diversity.

**Follow-ups:** The query has no size but the profile does—do you add a hard filter automatically? Promo prices jitter so the filter price and the displayed price disagree—which wins? Vectors expand “red” to burgundy—is that a hit or a second confirmation?

**Extended questions:**
- Hard filters inside the recall query versus after recall and before rank: how do empty-result rate and performance differ?
- Night running is a scene, not a field. How do you build a degrade recall path that does not depend on a generator?
- How do you make “rows in the result set that violate the price constraint” a must-be-zero online check?

**Reasoning:** The goal is to meet hard constraints without going empty; the constraints are a sparse catalog and bestseller bias. Split hard versus soft slots, filter hard conditions at recall, and put soft conditions in rank and explanation. Accept on constraint satisfaction and zero-result rate.

## DESIGN-011. Wikis across departments, tickets, and PDF files occupy a single corpus. Completing the answer takes three documents, and reviewers insist each sentence can be opened against the original span. How would you join citation alignment, a halt when nothing is answerable, and bounded multi-hop retrieval?

**Answer:** Enterprise document QA often fails because one top-k cannot hold the reasoning chain. Use bounded iterative retrieval: rewrite, fetch seed chunks, extract missing entities, run a second and third query with source-type filters, then give generation about 6–10 anchored chunks. After generation, align every sentence to a chunk id and offset and drop sentences that do not resolve. If a required chunk is still missing after three hops, stop the whole item, list what was found and what is still missing, and do not assemble a merely complete-looking answer.
